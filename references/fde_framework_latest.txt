Problem: Ephrata’s caregiver availability and after-hours call routing problems kept returning after Zingage had closed the tickets. The customer was still manually checking and correcting things. Meanwhile, the same team needed to offboard Durant and respond to Medford’s restart request.
Thought process: I started by separating what the records proved from what we were assuming. A successful afternoon test or a connected integration didn’t prove the overnight workflow worked consistently. The clearest gap was that we had closed technical tickets without verifying the customer’s original outcome.
How I prioritized: I put Ephrata’s routing investigation first because calls were reaching the wrong person. I also protected Durant’s agreed exit date and regular support coverage. For Medford, I planned scoping and a proposal before committing to a restart.
What I built: I turned that into a ten-business-day plan with named owners, hours, prerequisites, and required evidence for each task. The workbook connected the work log to daily and overall capacity checks, so changing an allocation would expose conflicts. I also wrote the diagnosis, execution handoff, customer communication drafts, and an eleven-step playbook.
Key tradeoff: Engineering was the tightest constraint: Ephrata could use only nine hours without escalation. I budgeted three for routing and six for the WellSky investigation. I also reduced overlapping observation effort, bringing Ephrata from 55 to 47 hours and freeing eight hours for exceptions.
how I defined success: A fix needed technical evidence, sustained observation, and the customer’s confirmation that routine manual corrections were no longer necessary. If those conditions weren’t met, recovery stayed open with a clear next action.
Outcome: We completed and versioned the assessment package in GitHub, tested the workbook’s capacity and evidence checks, and packaged the three submission deliverables. The outcome was an executable, tested proposal. Live repairs and customer acceptance were still pending.
takeaway: I learned to connect technical work to the customer’s actual goal, make resource tradeoffs explicit, and define the evidence needed for completion before starting execution.
